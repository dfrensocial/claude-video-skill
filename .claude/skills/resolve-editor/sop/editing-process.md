# The studio's editing process (source of truth)

This is Dhanush's own process. The skill follows it step by step. Where this and any other file disagree, this wins.

1. **Upload raw footage to the Resolve timeline.** Import everything, one bin per job, keep originals untouched.
2. **Trim, cut, place in the right order.** Read the transcript like an editor: remove stutters, repeats, flubs, dead air, false starts; pick the best take of each line; find what is missing (script lines with no take) and where parts must join so the whole reads as one piece. Use the script/brief for order if provided.
3. **Add B-roll.** Cover jump cuts, illustrate nouns/claims/actions, vary the visual every 2-4 s on talking-head.
4. **Check B-roll alignment and colour.** Starts/ends on the right word, no mismatch with the spoken line, colour palette matches the A-roll and brand.
5. **Add text.** Hook text, key-phrase emphasis, captions if requested, CTA. Brand fonts/colours, safe zones, high-quality entrances/exits.
6. **Add SFX.** Whooshes on transitions/text, hits on emphasis, subtle risers; music ducked under voice.
7. **Review and export** (added). Watch the render via frames + QC; fix; export requested formats.

Quality bar: motion graphics, text overlays, effects, entrances and exits must look premium - eased motion, no default-looking presets, consistent timing. Short generated clips (hook/stat/CTA cards) may be attached.

While cutting, "actively looking at the content" means: wherever the speaker stuttered, repeated, erred, skipped something, or where two parts merge - decide it from the transcript and timing, flag what cannot be decided.
